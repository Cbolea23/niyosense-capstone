## 2. THE "GOLDEN THREAD" ARCHITECTURE (FACULTY RULE #1)

During both lecture sessions, Engr. Maaño identified the **broken thread** as the primary reason capstone manuscripts receive conditional passes or re-defense verdicts. Panelists immediately cross-reference your Specific Objectives against your Results and Conclusions before reading anything else.

```
Specific Objectives (Chapter 1, Sec 1.3.2)
         │  (Every objective starts with an action verb)
         ▼
Operational Methods (Chapter 4 / 5)
         │  (Explains HOW that specific objective was built/configured)
         ▼
Results & Discussion (Chapter 5, Sec 5.1–5.N)
         │  (Presents the empirical EVIDENCE that the objective was achieved)
         ▼
Conclusions (Chapter 6, Item 1–N)
         │  (Delivers the definitive VERDICT: Was it achieved? What is the proof?)
         ▼
Recommendations (Chapter 6)
            (Actionable next steps based on findings and limitations)
```

### The 3 Golden Thread Rules:
1. **Strict 1-to-1-to-1 Correspondence:**
   - If there are $N$ Specific Objectives in Chapter 1, there must be **exactly $N$ Result subsections in Chapter 5** (numbered 5.1 to 5.N), and **exactly $N$ numbered conclusion statements in Chapter 6**.
   - No phantom objectives (results without an objective in Chapter 1).
   - No stray conclusions (conclusions not answering an objective).
2. **Infinitive to Past Tense Transformation:**
   - *Proposal (Chapter 1):* “To train two independent YOLOv8-cls models...”
   - *Methods (Chapter 4):* “The two independent YOLOv8-cls models were trained...”
   - *Results (Chapter 5):* “The trained visual and acoustic models achieved 94.1% combined accuracy...”
   - *Conclusions (Chapter 6):* “The dual-model architecture successfully classified young coconut maturity with 94.1% accuracy on mobile edge devices.”
3. **Substantive Result Headings (No Infinitives in Chapter 5):**
   - Section titles in Chapter 5 must describe the technical domain or engineering output (e.g., *“5.3 Results of Dual-Model Training and Late Fusion”*), **NEVER** an infinitive statement like *“5.3 To train two models”*.

## 3. MANUSCRIPT PRELIMINARY PAGES & FORMATTING MANDATES

### 1. Title Page & Researcher Profile
* Approved Title Case format. Avoid overly generic titles (e.g., “Coconut App”); state the core technical solution, key algorithm, and operational beneficiary.
* Proponents listed alphabetically by surname: `Surname, First Name M.I.`
* Degree format: `Bachelor of Science in Information Technology (Web and Mobile Application)`.
* Date format: Month and Year of Final Defense (e.g., `October 2026` or `May 2027`).

### 2. Approval Sheet
* Leave the final defense grade **blank** (`__________`). The oral examination committee fills this in during closed deliberation.
* Do **not** have the approval sheet signed before defense; it is signed only after all post-defense panel revisions and grammar certifications are completed.

### 3. Abstract (STRICT RULES — Timestamp 17:39–20:25 of Video 1)
* **Length:** Exactly one continuous paragraph of roughly **200 to 250 words**.
* **Page Budget:** Must fit on **EXACTLY ONE PAGE** together with the metadata header block.
* **Prohibited Elements:**
  - ❌ NO multiple paragraphs
  - ❌ NO citations or bracketed references `[n]`
  - ❌ NO figures, charts, or images
  - ❌ NO display mathematical equations
  - ❌ NO bulleted lists or sub-headings
* **Mandatory 5-Sentence Microcosm Flow:**
  1. *Problem (1–2 sentences):* The existing manual problem, human error, sensory fatigue, and financial loss in the local agricultural/enterprise context.
  2. *Major Objective (1 sentence):* What system was designed, developed, and evaluated.
  3. *Methodology (1 sentence):* Core development framework, edge ML algorithms, audio/vision processing, and cloud backend tools.
  4. *Quantitative Results (1–2 sentences with ACTUAL NUMBERS):* Real empirical performance metrics (e.g., *“achieved 94.1% classification accuracy, sub-450ms latency, 100% black-box test pass rate, and an overall ISO/IEC 25010 mean rating of 4.68 (Excellent)”*).
  5. *Conclusion / Impact (1 sentence):* Significance, operational efficiency, and commercial dispute prevention.
* **Index Terms:** 4 to 6 keywords in alphabetical order:
  `Index Terms — Acoustic signal processing, coconut maturity grading, dual-model machine learning, edge computing, ISO/IEC 25010, plantation management.`

### 4. Preliminary Lists
* Every preliminary list must be placed on its own page using explicit Word page breaks:
  - Table of Contents
  - Table of Figures (automated via Word `Insert Table of Figures`)
  - List of Tables (automated via Word `Insert Table of Figures -> Table`)
* Body text must never be styled as Heading 1/2/3 to prevent whole paragraphs from polluting the Table of Contents.

## 4. CHAPTER-BY-CHAPTER TECHNICAL DRAFTING REQUIREMENTS

### Chapter 1: Introduction
* **1.1 Project Context (Funnel Structure: 2–3 pages max):**
  - *Global:* Broader industry trends, agricultural non-destructive testing (NDT), automated grading.
  - *National (Philippines):* Importance of the Philippine coconut industry (3.6M hectares), Quezon Province as agricultural hub.
  - *Local / Stakeholder Grove:* Direct operational realities at Dr. Nona D. Nagares' plantation:
    - *Buko* (mature young coconut, thick spoon-soft meat, premium price ₱12–16) vs. *Malauhog* (premature, thin translucent meat, discounted price ₱5–8).
    - The sensory overlap problem: green outer husks look identical.
    - Sorter fatigue under tropical heat causing high misclassification.
    - Commercial pricing disputes and revenue leakage ($\Delta V = V_{\text{est}} - V_{\text{actual}}$).
    - Rural connectivity constraints (zero cellular signal in groves).
  - *End with:* Why on-device edge computing and dual-model audio-visual AI is the exact technological solution. Do not list system features here.
* **1.2 Purpose and Description:** 1–2 paragraphs bridging the problem context to the objectives.
* **1.3 Objectives of the Study:** SMART Main Objective + 4 to 7 measurable Specific Objectives.
* **1.4 Significance of the Study:** Concrete direct beneficiaries: Plantation Owners, On-Site Farm Managers, Commercial Buyers/Processors, Academic Community.
* **1.5 Scope and Delimitations:** Clear boundary setting:
  - *Scope:* Young green coconuts, binary maturity (*Buko* vs. *Malauhog*), cluster (*bungkos*) representative sampling (8–15 nuts), offline Android execution, cloud dashboard on Render.
  - *Delimitations (Crucial defense shield):* Excludes mature brown dry nuts (*niyog* for copra), excludes automated tree climbing robots, excludes biochemical Brix sugar titration, relies on a Controlled Grading Station.

### Chapter 2: Theoretical Framework & Literature Review
* **2.1 Theoretical Framework / Concept Map:** Visual diagram linking problem $ightarrow$ underlying scientific principles $ightarrow$ tech stack $ightarrow$ outcomes. Must include an explanatory paragraph below the figure.
* **2.2 Review of Related Literature (RRL):**
  - Target 50+ peer-reviewed sources (IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, ACM, Scopus, Google Scholar).
  - Organized by **THEMES**, not chronological author dumps.
  - Strict IEEE format: `[1]`, `[2]`. Synthesize multiple authors per paragraph.
  - Conclude with the **Synthesis & Research Gap** explaining what makes your system distinct.
* **2.3 Review of Related Systems (Comparative Matrix):**
  - Table comparing at least 3 existing systems/studies against your proposed system across critical feature criteria.
* **2.4 Conceptual Framework (IPO Model with Feedback Loop):**
  - Input $ightarrow$ Process $ightarrow$ Output $ightarrow$ Feedback Loop for continuous optimization.
* **2.5 Operational Definition of Terms:** Alphabetical operational definitions (how terms are used in your system).
* **2.6 Acronyms:** Alphabetical list formatted as `ACRONYM – Full Name`.

### Chapter 3: Operational Framework
* **3.1 Software Requirements / Constraints:** Table listing software, version, purpose, and constraints.
* **3.2 Hardware Requirements / Constraints:** Table listing development and deployment hardware specifications.
* **3.3 Data Gathering Procedure & Ethics Compliance:**
  - Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for data collection.
  - Ground-truth validation (destructive cross-section caliper measurements).
  - Compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173).

### Chapter 4: Description of Methods or Approach
* **4.1 Requirements Definition:** User personas, gap analysis, and functional/non-functional requirements.
* **4.2 Design Phases:** System architecture diagrams, database schemas, and mathematical algorithms (e.g., pure-Dart STFT DSP pipeline, 3-gate OOD protocol, 70/30 late fusion combiner, cluster sampling formula).
* **4.3 Description of Prototype:** Walkthrough of mobile screens and web dashboard modules.
* **4.4 Development Phases:** Development lifecycle (Agile Scrum or Agile Kanban) detailing phase outputs.
* **4.5 Testing Procedure and Evaluation Procedure:**
  - Black-box functional test case schema (`TC-ID`, `Objective`, `Procedure`, `Expected Result`, `Actual Result`, `Pass/Fail`).
  - ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation procedure and Likert scale formula.
* **4.6 Implementation Plan:** Deployment pipeline, APK distribution, and handover plan.
* **4.7 Capstone Workplan:** Actual chronological schedule of project execution.
* **4.8 Ethics Consideration:** Informed consent, anonymization, and environmental sustainability.

### Chapter 5: Results and Discussion (New for Final Manuscript)
* **The Heart of Final Defense:** Must present the empirical EVIDENCE that every Specific Objective was achieved.
* **Structure:** Organized strictly by Specific Objective (`5.1` to `5.N`).
* **The Mandatory 3-Step Cadence for Every Subsection:**
  1. **Present:** Display tables, diagrams, or charts with proper captions.
  2. **Interpret:** State what the numbers mean (e.g., *“latency decreased from 2.4s to 318ms; classification accuracy reached 94.1%”*).
  3. **Discuss:** Explain *why* that result occurred from a scientific/engineering standpoint and compare against RRL literature. Avoid *“hilaw na discussion”* (bare screenshot dumping).

### Chapter 6: Conclusions and Recommendations (New for Final Manuscript)
* **6.1 Summary of Findings:** Succinct recap of major empirical milestones.
* **6.2 Conclusions:** **Exactly $N$ numbered conclusions answering the $N$ Specific Objectives 1-to-1.**
  - Direct verdict: Was the objective achieved? What is the proof?
  - Written in the past tense. No new data or citations.
* **6.3 Recommendations:** Actionable recommendations grounded in findings and technical limitations:
  - To the Organization / Beneficiary (Plantation Owner)
  - To Operational Users (Farm Managers / Sorters)
  - To Future Researchers (Unexplored cultivars, automated actuators, predictive shelf life)

### References & Standalone Appendices Document
* **References:** Strict IEEE format, ordered chronologically by first in-text citation `[1]`, `[2]`.
* **Appendices Document:** Standalone companion volume containing:
  - Appendix A: Key Source Code Snippets
  - Appendix B: Complete Black-Box Test Cases Matrix (TC-01 to TC-28)
  - Appendix C: ISO/IEC 25010 Survey Instrument & Tabulated Data Sheets
  - Appendix D: Stakeholder Consent Letters & Institutional Ethics Clearances
  - Appendix E: Field Operator & Web Management User Manuals
  - Appendix F: One-Page Professional Curriculum Vitae of Proponents

## 8. PANELIST Q&A PSYCHOLOGY & COMMON TRAP QUESTIONS

Engr. Maaño highlighted the standard questions panels ask computing students:

### Trap 1: “Why didn't you just use visual inspection? Why add audio?”
* **The Winning Answer:** “In young coconuts, the external green husks of mature *Buko* and premature *Malauhog* are morphologically and visually near-identical ($p = 0.318$). Visual models plateau at 88.6% accuracy because the outer husk does not reflect internal solid meat thickness. Acoustic tapping excites the internal fluid-filled cavity; as meat thickness increases from $1.24	ext{ mm}$ to $4.82	ext{ mm}$, acoustic resonance drops from $1,840	ext{ Hz}$ to $1,120	ext{ Hz}$ ($p < 0.0001$). Fusing both modalities via our 70/30 late fusion rule boosts accuracy to 94.1%.”

### Trap 2: “Why did you build your own DSP engine in Dart instead of using Python Librosa on a server?”
* **The Winning Answer:** “Commercial coconut plantations in Quezon Province are situated in rural groves with zero cellular connectivity. Cloud-dependent APIs fail immediately in the field. By engineering the complete 300Hz HPF, STFT, and 128-band Mel-filterbank directly in pure Dart, our system runs 100% offline on standard Android smartphones in 318 milliseconds, achieving an $R^2 = 0.994$ correlation against desktop Librosa.”

### Trap 3: “What prevents a worker from scanning a green apple or a green table?”
* **The Winning Answer:** “Our deterministic 3-Gate Out-of-Distribution Protocol. Gate 1 evaluates HSV color boundaries, luminance exposure, and 3D spherical curvature roughness ($\sigma_{	ext{green}} \ge 6.5$), locking the shutter on flat surfaces or non-coconut hues. Gate 2 enforces the 3-knock acoustic cadence, rejecting ambient speech or silent clicks. In empirical testing across 100 foreign objects, the protocol achieved a 100% rejection rate with zero false positives.”

### Trap 4: “How can scanning one coconut grade an entire truckload?”
* **The Winning Answer:** “In plantation agronomy, coconuts grow in clusters (*bungkos*) attached to a single stalk, sharing identical flowering dates, vascular nutrient supply, and physiological maturation. Field testing confirmed that nuts within the same *bungkos* exhibit uniform meat thickness. Our Cluster Representative Sampling profiles 8 to 15 nuts per bunch in one scan, reducing commercial sorting labor time by 88.6%.”

  *“Huwag kayong maglalagay ng screenshot tapos sasabihin niyo ‘Figure 5 shows the login screen.’ Tapos wala na? Napakahilaw nyan! You must present, interpret the numbers, and discuss why the system behaved that way compared to related studies.”*
* **On Abstract Length:**  
  *“Ang abstract natin is strictly one single paragraph. Huwag gagawing essay na may tatlong paragraphs. Dapat kasya sa isang page kasama ang table sa itaas, walang citations, at may complete numbers ng results.”*
* **On Grammar Editor Fees:**  
  *“Kaya natin inihihiwalay ang appendices sa separate document ay para hindi kayo gumastos ng libo-libo sa grammar editor. Ang ibabayad niyo sa grammarian ay yung main manuscript lang, hindi kasama ang 50 pages ng source code at test cases.”*
* **On Video Demos:**  
  *“Mag-record kayo ng 5 to 7 minutes na video demo ng working system. Kasi kapag defense day, pwedeng mag-loko ang Wi-Fi, pwedeng mag-crash ang phone. Kapag may video kayo, siguradong makikita ng panel ang system niyo nang buo.”*
